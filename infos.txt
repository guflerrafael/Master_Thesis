Outline and Scope:

Wildfires are dynamic spatiotemporal phenomena whose evolution is influenced by vegetation, topography, weather, and fire behaviour. Satellite-derived Fire Radiative Power (FRP) provides valuable information about the location and intensity of active fires, making it a promising data source for understanding fire dynamics. Recent advances in deep learning offer new opportunities to exploit such spatiotemporal observations, in particular for extracting meaningful information about wildfire evolution and supporting fire monitoring and management (Xu et al.,2025).
The main objective of this project is to investigate deep learning approaches for analysing the spatiotemporal evolution of satellite-derived FRP observations and their application to wildfire characterization. Particular emphasis will be placed on developing models capable of learning complex spatial and temporal patterns associated with fire propagation and relating these patterns to key wildfire attributes, including the estimation of ignition location.
Specific objectives include: (i) developing a spatiotemporal dataset integrating FRP observations with complementary environmental information; (ii) designing and evaluating deep learning architectures for modelling wildfire evolution from sequential satellite observations; (iii) assessing different modelling strategies for extracting information from FRP time series; (iv) quantifying the contribution of environmental variables such as topography, land cover, fuel characteristics, and meteorological conditions to model performance; and (v) evaluating the robustness, accuracy, and interpretability of the proposed methods across different wildfire scenarios.
The expected outcome is an interpretable framework capable of reconstructing wildfire origins from remotely sensed observations, contributing to improved understanding of fire behaviour and supporting future operational tools for wildfire monitoring, reconstruction, and decision-making.

The project will begin with the compilation and preprocessing of historical wildfire events in Portugal, where reliable ignition records are available from national fire databases. These records will be combined with satellite-derived Fire Radiative Power (FRP) observations, organized into spatiotemporal sequences describing fire evolution from the earliest detectable stages onwards. In this regard records of FRP as derived from the SEVIRI sensor on-board Meteosat Second Generation geostationary satellites are especially promising taking into account the temporal resolution (images acquired every 15 minutes). Complementary datasets, including digital elevation models, land cover and vegetation maps, fuel characteristics, and meteorological variables (e.g., wind, temperature, humidity, and precipitation) obtained from national and European services, will be harmonized onto a common spatial and temporal framework. If the methodology proves successful, the approach will be extended to the European scale using harmonized datasets, enabling its evaluation across a wider range of fire regimes and environmental conditions.
The first modelling stage will develop a ConvLSTM architecture (Shi et al., 2015), treating FRP observations as temporal image sequences to capture the spatial evolution of fire fronts. A second architecture combining CNN-based spatial feature extraction with a Transformer encoder will then be implemented to model long-range temporal dependencies (Vaswani et al., 2017). Both approaches will be trained to estimate ignition coordinates and ignition probability maps.
Model performance will be evaluated using independent wildfire events, comparing localization accuracy, computational efficiency, and generalization across different fire sizes, vegetation types, and environmental settings. Finally, explainable artificial intelligence techniques will be applied to identify the spatiotemporal FRP patterns driving model predictions, providing insights into wildfire dynamics and supporting the future integration of deep learning methods into operational wildfire reconstruction and post-fire analysis.
